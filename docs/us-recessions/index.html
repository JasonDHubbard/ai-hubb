    1. Recessions and political parties

    Party refers to the president in office (see methodology for how start/end presidents are assigned). ■ Democratic■ Republican

    Recessions by party: in office at start vs. at end

    Counts of the 34 recessions by the party of the president in office in the start month and in the trough (end) month.

    Average and median duration by party at start

    NBER peak-to-trough length in months, grouped by the party in office when the recession started.

    Total recession-months by party

    “By start party” credits each recession's whole duration to the party in office at its start. “Month-by-month” credits each recession month to the party of the president in office on the 1st of that month (derived from the inauguration dates in the file).

    Normalised: exposure-adjusted rates

    When in the term did recessions start?

    Bucketed by the president's complete months in office at the recession start month (Year 1 = 0–11 months, Year 2 = 12–23, …). Year 5+ only applies to presidents who served beyond one 4-year term.

    Preceding president's party vs. start party

    “Preceding president” is the administration immediately before the president in office at the start (not necessarily the one in office in the prior recession).

    Timeline: recession start year vs. duration

    Each point is a recession, plotted at its start month and coloured by the party in office at the start. Hover for details.

    Start party × end party

    Diagonal cells: started and ended under the same party. Off-diagonal cells: the presidency changed party during the recession.

    2. Recessions and global / other causes

    The file lists 2–4 brief “possible causes” per recession plus a World Bank global-recession overlap flag. Cause categories below are derived by keyword matching on the cause text (method and rules shown at the bottom of this section); one recession can carry several categories.

    Cause-category frequency, split by party at start

    Number of recessions tagged with each category (not number of cause strings).

    Cause category × party (and global overlap)

    Percentages are of each party's recessions (11 Democratic-start, 23 Republican-start). “Global overlap” = share of tagged recessions flagged as overlapping a World Bank global recession, among those with a non-null flag.

    World Bank global-recession overlap

    Overlap flag is null for the four earliest recessions (1857–1870); these are shown as “n/a” and not counted as “no”.

    Global overlap vs. not: duration and stock drawdown

    Averages computed only over recessions where the value is present (n shown). Drawdowns are separate index peak-to-trough moves and may extend outside the recession.
